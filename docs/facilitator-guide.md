# Facilitator guide

**Audience:** general employees; no technical prerequisites. **Duration:** about 12 minutes individually, or 20 minutes with discussion.

## Learning objectives

After completing the game, the learner should be able to:

1. Identify personal details in captions, images, and comments that make a password guessable.
2. Explain how details from multiple social platforms form a combined digital footprint.
3. Choose unique, randomly generated passwords and MFA as practical account protections.
4. Recognize a workplace credential exposure and choose to report it for rotation.

## Learning design

Each case follows observation → inference → consequence → action. Learners receive explanatory feedback on relevant clues and distractions, then apply a security principle to a concrete response decision. The password puzzle creates the memorable moment; the protection decision supplies the transfer to work.

Glen covers personal details. Shanti covers cross-platform aggregation and reuse. Kyle covers aliases and MFA. Lance covers background details and reporting an exposed work secret. Case difficulty increases, but all cases are available so learners can choose their own path.

The transcript is an equal way to play, not a penalty-bearing hint. It transcribes relevant visual content and clarifies ambiguous source details. The historical source media uses inconsistent dates; the prompts explicitly treat it as an archived fictional file. Scoring rewards completion and exploration without timers or lost lives.

## Facilitation

Before playing, ask: “Could someone infer something private from a perfectly ordinary post?” Avoid asking learners to share passwords or personal account details.

After each case, ask: “Which part of the post did you overlook?” At the end, ask learners to choose one action: replace a reused password, turn on MFA, or review a photo for work information before posting.

Debrief with the playbook. Emphasize that password managers, unique passwords, and MFA reduce risk without relying on secrecy about normal life events. For workplace exposures, use your organization’s reporting path.

## Measurement and rollout

Completion requires correct evidence choices, a solved fictional password, and a correct protection decision in each case. The downloadable report records local practice completion and XP. It is not identity-verified and can be edited. It must not be used as a compliance attestation.

This version has no LMS, SCORM, xAPI, central reporting, authentication, or administrator dashboard. Those integrations need the target platform and reporting requirements. Progress is device/browser-specific and can be lost if browser data is cleared.

Validate Safari home-screen installation and Android installation on physical devices, check with the organization’s preferred assistive technology, and review your organization’s completion requirements.
